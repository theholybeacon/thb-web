## The one-line pitch

> Most Bible apps hand you a verse and move on.

The Holy Beacon is built for people who want to actually *work through*
Scripture: a plan to follow, three different ways to take in each chapter, the
people behind the text, and somewhere to keep what they find.

## Free, no account needed

### Bible Explorer

**What it does.** Read the Bible online in **404 translations** across dozens of
languages, with no sign-up and no paywall. Pick a translation, pick a book, pick
a chapter, and read.

**The problem it solves.** Most free Bible readers give you one or two
translations and bury them in clutter. Someone who wants to compare how the
Reina-Valera and the Berean Standard render the same passage usually has to open
two sites.

**What's distinctive.**
- The whole catalogue is free and needs no account — that is the front door to
  the product, not a trial.
- A **"Listenable only"** filter, so a reader who wants narration can see at a
  glance which translations offer it.
- Because the catalogue is large and hard to judge, the product **recommends one
  translation per language** and says why in a single line — so a newcomer isn't
  handed 36 near-identical English options and left to guess.
- Chapter navigation carries you across book boundaries: the end of Malachi
  leads into Matthew, rather than dead-ending.
- Every chapter and every character has its own permanent link, so you can send
  someone straight to the passage.

### Bible Characters

**What it does.** A browsable A–Z directory of **3,067 people named in
Scripture**, each with their own page: their names and alternate spellings,
approximate dates, and every verse in the Bible that mentions them, grouped by
book.

For characters whose profile has been written, the page also carries a plain
overview, why they matter, a timeline of events in their life, and their
relationships to other people — **with every statement cited to a specific
verse**.

**The problem it solves.** You read a name in a chapter and have no idea who it
is or where else they appear. Looking it up means leaving the passage and
trusting whatever a search engine returns.

**What's distinctive.** This is the feature the product calls **"Scripture in
HD"** — *know who you are reading about, not just what happened.* The citation
discipline is the point: profiles are checked against the character's real verse
mentions, so a claim that cannot be traced to a verse does not appear. The
promise in the product's own words is *"Every claim is cited to a real verse
mention — nothing invented."*

Names also light up **inside the verse text itself** while you read, so you can
go from a name in the passage to who they were without losing your place. (The
names are visible to everyone; following the link is a Premium feature.)

### Reading progress — "Your Journey"

**What it does.** Tracks every chapter you have read, heard, or typed, and shows
it as a grid of all **1,189 chapters** of the Bible. Darker squares are chapters
you have been back to. It counts how much of the Bible you have covered, how
many books you have finished, how you took each chapter in, and how many
complete times through you have made. **14 milestone badges** mark the way —
first chapter, first book, the Torah, the Gospels, a quarter, a half, the whole
New Testament, a full lap, and more.

**The problem it solves.** People start reading the Bible constantly and lose
track of where they got to and how far they have come. Progress becomes a vague
feeling instead of something you can see.

**What's distinctive.**
- It counts progress **wherever you started** — reading in one translation and
  listening in another still counts toward the same chapter.
- The denominator is fixed at the 66-book canon, so your percentage means the
  same thing as everyone else's and is genuinely comparable.
- You can **share your journey as a public page or as a vertical image** made for
  social — and sharing is off by default until you turn it on.

### Verse of the day and reading streaks

**What it does.** A hand-picked verse each day (**105 in the rotation**), plus a
day-count streak that grows as long as you keep showing up. Optional daily
reminder emails, including a nudge when a streak is about to break.

**The problem it solves.** The hardest part of Bible reading is showing up
tomorrow.

**What's distinctive.** Everyone sees the same verse on the same day, so it is a
shared daily moment rather than a random pick.

## Premium

### AI study plans — the heart of the product

**What it does.** Describe what you want to learn — *"What does the Bible say
about forgiveness?"*, *"Laziness as a sin"*, *"the parables of Jesus"* — set how
long you want it to take and how deep you want to go, and get a structured plan
back: passage by passage, session by session, each step with its own reading and
an explanation of why it is there.

The two dials are:
- **Length** — anywhere from **5 minutes to a full year**.
- **Depth** — from a beginner-friendly introduction to a theological deep dive.

**The problem it solves.** Plenty of people want to study a topic properly and
have no idea which passages to read or in what order. The alternative is a
generic 30-day plan that has nothing to do with the question they actually have,
or nothing at all.

**What's distinctive.** The plan is built around *your* question rather than
picked off a shelf, and you can regenerate it if the first attempt misses. As you
work through it, your progress is tracked step by step and chapter by chapter,
and you can pick up exactly where you left off.

### Ready-made reading plans

For readers who don't want to describe anything, two complete plans are ready to
adopt. Both cover **all 1,189 chapters, each exactly once**:

- **"Just Read the Bible"** — **102 readings**, in the order the events actually
  happened. Job sits with the patriarchs, Chronicles beside Samuel and Kings,
  each prophet in the reign he preached to, and Paul's letters woven into the
  journeys of Acts.
- **"From Cover to Cover"** — **66 readings**, one whole book at a time, Genesis
  straight through to Revelation. Nothing rearranged, and never any doubt about
  where you are.

Adopting a plan copies it into your own account in your own translation, so your
progress and notes are yours.

### Three ways through every chapter

The same chapter, three different ways to take it in — this is the product's
signature, summed up as **Read · Listen · Type**.

**Read.** A clean, distraction-free page with adjustable text size, light or
dark, laid out so poetry reads as poetry and paragraphs as paragraphs.

**Listen.** The passage read aloud in a natural voice, with **verse-by-verse
highlighting** that follows along as it plays. Two voices, one female and one
male. Playback speed and volume are adjustable, audio can be **downloaded for
offline**, and — because it lives at the top of the app — **it keeps playing with
your screen off**, on a commute or through a car.

> *"Hear every passage read aloud in a natural voice — with your screen off, on
> your commute, or through your car."*

**Type.** You type the passage out, character by character, and it grades you
live on **accuracy and words per minute**. It accepts a plain apostrophe where
the translation has a curly one, so you are never fighting punctuation. The
pitch is the sharpest line in the product:

> **"Type Scripture out word by word with live speed and accuracy. Slower than
> reading, and that is exactly the point."**

**The problem all three solve.** Reading Scripture silently is one mode, and it
suits one kind of attention. Someone with a 40-minute commute, someone who
retains nothing from skimming, and someone sitting down for focused study need
three different things from the same chapter.

**What's distinctive.** Typing mode has essentially no equivalent elsewhere. It
turns reading into something you cannot do passively — you cannot skim a chapter
you are typing.

### Notes that stay attached to the verse

**What it does.** Write a note on a single verse, a chapter, a whole book, or an
entire translation. Every note is searchable in one place, and each one links
straight back to the passage it came from.

**The problem it solves.** Notes in a separate app lose their anchor. Six months
later you have a thought and no idea which passage prompted it.

**What's distinctive.** The note belongs to the verse, not to a notebook. Open
the chapter and your notes are already there.

### Community discussion

**What it does.** Readers can post on any passage or any character, labelled as a
**comment, a fact, an analysis, or a correction**. Posts can be replied to,
voted on, and flagged. There is a feed of everything the community is discussing
across every translation, filterable by translation, book, chapter, verse, kind
and author.

**What's distinctive.** Typing a contribution as a *fact* or a *correction*
rather than an opinion sets a different tone from a general comment section — and
it means a correction to a generated character profile has somewhere to go.

### Original-language word lookup

**What it does.** Highlight any word while reading and see what stands behind it
in the original Greek or Hebrew, along with an ordinary dictionary definition.

**What's distinctive — and this matters for how we talk about it.** The product
is explicit about how confident it is. Some translations have genuine
word-by-word scholarly mapping; for others the original word is a model's best
guess, and the product **says so on the screen** rather than presenting a guess
as scholarship. Do not describe this feature as authoritative for every
translation. The honest framing is: *scholarly where scholarship exists, clearly
labelled as inference where it doesn't.*

## Access, and the sponsorship program

**Explorer — free, forever.** Full Bible Explorer, all 404 translations, character
pages, reading progress, no account required.

**Premium — monthly or yearly** (yearly saves 20%). Everything above, plus AI
study plans, unlimited study sessions, narrated audio with offline download,
clickable character links inside verse text, notes, and community discussion.
New readers get a **free trial that does not ask for a card**.

**Premium can be gifted.** You can buy a subscription for someone by email, and
they claim it with a link.

**The sponsorship program.** This is genuinely unusual and worth leading with in
the right context. Readers who cannot afford Premium can **join a sponsorship
queue** — they see their position and an estimated wait — and paying members can
**sponsor a stranger** from that queue to clear it.

> *"Our community-driven sponsorship program connects those who want to support
> others with those who need access to The Holy Beacon."*

Nobody is turned away for not being able to pay. That is a story, not a feature
bullet.

## Available in English and Spanish

The entire interface is available in **English and Spanish**, fully translated —
**820 pieces of interface text in each**, with no gaps in either. Readers switch
language from a control in the app.

Separately, the product **recommends a best translation for six reading
languages**: English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese and Italian.

> Do not conflate the two. The *interface* is available in 2 languages. The
> *Bible text* is available in dozens, with a recommended pick in 6.

## Not features — don't claim these

- **No search across the Bible text yet.** Full-text search is on the roadmap,
  not shipped.
- **No side-by-side translation comparison yet.** Also roadmap.
- **No chapter summaries yet.** Roadmap.
- **No study circles or discussion boards.** A community feed exists; organised
  group study does not.
- **No dictation mode.** Three ways through a chapter, not four.
- **Narration does not exist for every chapter of every translation.** It is
  produced on demand and only for translations that are legally clear for it.
  Never promise audio for a specific passage without checking.
